A package-holiday price is a claim, not a fact. The headline number is a marketing
asset that can change between the moment you read it and the moment you pay, and
most of the "cheapest holiday" lists you will find are built from feed data nobody
checked.

Here is what to look at instead. None of it requires expertise — it requires five
minutes and the source page open next to the offer.

## The price has to be dated

An undated price is not a price. Suppliers re-price constantly, and a figure from
three weeks ago is a guess dressed as a fact. Before you believe a number, find
the date it was checked and the date the travel window closes.

If neither is stated, assume the offer is stale. That is not cynicism; it is the
only safe reading of a number with no timestamp.

## The price has to be per person *and* a total

Most operators advertise per person and charge for two. Some show a total and
quote per person underneath. Both are legitimate; a number with no unit is not.

Work out the total before you compare anything. A €549 per-person offer for two
travellers is not cheaper than a €980 total offer — it is €1,098, and the second
one was the better deal all along.

## Know what the price excludes

The gap between a headline price and the checkout price is usually one of these:

- **Baggage** — a checked bag on a return flight is routinely charged separately.
- **Transfers** — an airport 70 km from the resort is not the resort.
- **Tourist tax** — charged locally, per person, per night, in several destinations.
- **Board** — half board, all inclusive and room-only prices are not comparable.

An offer that states its exclusions is more trustworthy than a cheaper one that
does not, because you can do the arithmetic.

## Check the flight, not just the route

Two offers for the same resort at the same price are not the same offer. Look at:

- **The airport you actually depart from.** A "Munich" departure that requires a
  train to another city first is not a Munich departure.
- **The arrival time.** A flight landing at 23:40 costs you a first night.
- **The connection.** One stop can add four hours in each direction.
- **The days.** Seven nights landing on a Thursday is not the same week as seven
  nights landing on a Saturday, and can be materially cheaper.

## Distrust the discount, trust the date

"40% off" is measured against a price the operator chose. A percentage off an
inflated baseline is not a saving. What matters is whether this specific trip, on
these specific dates, is cheaper than the same trip elsewhere — and whether the
dates you can actually travel are still available.

Seasonality explains most of what looks like a deal. The same hotel in the second
week of September can be 30% cheaper than in the first week of August without
anyone discounting anything.
