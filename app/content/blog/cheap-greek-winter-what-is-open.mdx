A cheap Greek week in December is cheap for one reason: the season is over.

That is not a trick and it is not a scam. It is the entire explanation, and once you
accept it the question stops being "is this a good deal" and becomes "what exactly am I
buying".

## The hotel is the product, or the place is

There are two different holidays on sale at the same price, and they are not
interchangeable.

The first is built around a resort. The hotel is the point: the pool, the beach in front
of it, the strip of tavernas along the road, the animation programme in the evening. In
winter most of that is shut. The strips at Malia and Hersonissos on Crete, or Faliraki on
Rhodes, are built for a season and largely empty outside it. A reseller will still sell
you a room there in January. You will arrive somewhere that has been packed up.

The second is built around a town. Chania, Heraklion, Rhodes town, Athens. These are
places where people live and work all year. The bakery opens. The pharmacy opens. The
museum opens. The seafront is windy and the water is cold, but nothing has been put away.

The test is one question: **if the hotel closed tomorrow, would there still be a reason to
be here?** If the answer is no, you are buying a resort in its off season — and you should
know that before you pay for it rather than after you arrive.

## What "open" actually means

"Open" is not one thing. Four separate things have to be true, and a cheap offer usually
tells you about none of them.

**Does the hotel itself take your dates?** Check the property's own website, not only the
reseller's page. A hotel that is closed for the season often still appears in a package
search, because the inventory is being sold by an intermediary. If the hotel's own booking
calendar is blank for your week, believe it.

**Is the street outside alive?** Recent reviews are the cheapest evidence available. Look
for reviews from the last eight weeks, not last August. A handful of winter reviews
mentioning a closed pool or a shuttered seafront is worth more than any star rating.

**How do you get from the airport?** Winter ferry schedules are thinner than summer ones,
and weather cancellations are more common. If any part of your plan depends on a boat,
read the operator's winter timetable rather than the summer one that ranks first.

**Is the building warm?** Greek buildings are built to shed heat, not to hold it. Many
have no central heating at all, and a December evening indoors can feel colder than the
same temperature outside. Ask the hotel directly whether the room is heated. Air
conditioning is a different machine and does not answer the question.

## What the price is actually telling you

A winter Greek week is genuinely cheaper, and the saving is real. It is also buying a
different product from the one in the brochure photographs. That is fine if you want a
quiet town, a warm coat and a cheap flight. It is a bad purchase if you wanted the beach.

The honest version of the deal is this: you are trading the beach for the price. Decide
whether that trade is worth it before you book, not after you land.
